4 Communication Styles

Click each card to reveal deeper traits.

Passive
"Whatever you think is best."
Avoids conflict at all costs. Speaks softly, rarely disagrees, apologizes excessively. Underneath: fear of rejection, low self-worth. May harbor resentment that surfaces indirectly.
Aggressive
"This is how it's going to be."
Dominates conversations, interrupts, uses pointed gestures and intense eye contact. Underneath: insecurity masked as power. Needs to win to feel safe.
Passive-Aggressive
"Fine. Whatever." (it's not fine)
Expresses anger indirectly — sarcasm, silent treatment, "forgetting," backhanded compliments. Underneath: wants to express anger but fears direct confrontation.
Assertive
"Here's how I see it. What about you?"
Speaks clearly with calm confidence. Respects boundaries, maintains eye contact, listens actively. Underneath: high self-worth, emotional regulation, mutual respect.

️ Body Language Decoder

Click any body area to uncover hidden meanings.

🦶 Feet
  • Pointing toward you = interest/engagement
  • Pointing away = desire to leave
  • Tapping/fidgeting = anxiety or impatience
  • Interlaced around chair = discomfort
🦵 Legs
  • Spread wide = dominance / comfort
  • Crossed away = defensiveness
  • Bouncing = nervous energy
  • Knee clasp = ready to leave
🤲 Hands
  • Palms visible = openness, honesty
  • Steepling = confidence, authority
  • Rubbing/wringing = stress, self-soothing
  • Thumbs hidden = insecurity
Arms
  • Crossed high = barrier, defensiveness
  • Crossed low = self-soothing
  • Open + relaxed = receptiveness
  • Shoulders to ears = acute stress
️ Eyes
  • Pupil dilation = interest / attraction
  • Rapid blinking = discomfort / stress
  • Gaze aversion = processing or hiding
  • Brow flash = recognition / greeting
Face
  • Real smile = eyes crinkle (Duchenne)
  • Lip compression = stress / anger
  • Nostril flare = arousal / aggression
  • Head tilt = interest / trust

🧩 Emotional Intelligence Principles

Self-Awareness
Recognize your own emotions as they happen. You can't read others if you can't read yourself.
Self-Regulation
Pause before reacting. Emotional control prevents hijacking your perception of others.
️
Empathy
Feel with people, not just for them. True understanding comes from perspective-taking.
Social Skill
Navigate relationships with intention. Rapport is the gateway to reading anyone.

Key Techniques to Read People

01
Establish a Baseline
Know how someone acts when calm before judging deviations. One gesture means nothing — context is everything.
02
Look for Clusters
A single behavior is unreliable. 2–3 congruent signals form a real pattern you can trust.
03
Watch for Juxtaposition
When words and body contradict, believe the body. Nonverbal leaks are subconscious and honest.
04
Detect Sudden Changes
A shift in behavior = a shift in emotional state. That moment is where the truth lives.
05
Listen for What's Not Said
Pauses, omissions, deflections, and vague language reveal more than the words themselves.
06
Read the Eyes First
The eyes are the most expressive feature. Pupils, blinking, and gaze direction are windows into internal state.

Practical Exercises

Sharpen your perception with daily drills.

Exercise 1

The People-Watching Audit

Spend 5 minutes in a public space. Observe one person from feet to face. What do their feet say? Their hands? Their eyes? Write three observations.

Exercise 2

The Contradiction Hunt

In your next conversation, look for mismatches between words and body. Someone says "I'm fine" but has crossed arms and averted eyes. Note these juxtapositions.

Exercise 3

The Silence Game

In a conversation, let silence hang for 5 seconds. Watch what the other person does with their body during the pause. People reveal themselves in silence.

Exercise 4

Emotional Labeling Drill

Watch a movie or show on mute. Pause every 30 seconds and label the emotion of each character. Then replay with sound to check your accuracy.
